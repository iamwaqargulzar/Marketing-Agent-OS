# Crisis Communications

Use for an active reputation incident or a prepared response plan. Keep communications grounded in the incident owner's evidence.

## Triage

Distinguish a routine complaint, a localized escalating incident, a broad material disruption, and a safety/legal/regulatory emergency. These are planning categories, not a diagnosis or a statutory reporting rule. Route technical containment to the incident owner and consequential legal, regulatory, or safety statements to qualified reviewers.

During the initial response:

1. Record what happened, when it was observed, affected scope, verified impact, unknowns, and the source for each fact. Preserve evidence; do not reproduce sensitive customer data in drafts.
2. Assign one communications owner and identify who can confirm technical/customer facts. Pause only the scheduled messages the user authorizes pausing.
3. Prioritize direct help for affected people, a factual owned update, support guidance, and then broader media/social communication as appropriate to the incident. Do not assume every complaint needs a public statement.
4. Draft a holding statement with known facts, what the team is doing, what the affected audience should do, an accessible update location, and a realistic time for the next update. Avoid inventing causes, declaring resolution before validation, or promising outcomes the owner cannot support.
5. Separate technical remediation, customer remedies, communications approval, publication receipt, and observed response. Update the statement when new facts are confirmed; retain timestamps and corrections.

## Holding statement scaffold

At [verified time], we identified [verified issue]. It affects [confirmed scope; otherwise say this is still being assessed]. We are [confirmed action]. Affected customers can [supported help/action]. We will provide the next update at [owner-approved time] on [owned channel].

Replace every placeholder with supported information or omit the sentence. Use direct, empathetic language suited to the brand. Acknowledging confirmed impact need not speculate about fault. Obtain review where liability, regulated disclosures, personal data, or safety is involved.

## Close the loop

Publish or send only within the user's granted scope. Record the actual receipt, monitor meaningful questions and inaccuracies, and issue corrections promptly. After resolution, document the timeline, customer impact, remedy, and prevention work only as confirmed by the responsible teams.
