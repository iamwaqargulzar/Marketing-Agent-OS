# Competitive Sales Enablement

Use for battlecards, a live deal objection, or win-loss analysis. Ask which competitor or deal when it is missing rather than inventing one.

## Battlecards

Include when the competitor appears, why buyers consider it, where it is strong, where the user's product wins and for whom, discovery questions, sourced objection responses, when to qualify out, and the last verification date. Support advantages with buyer, CRM, call, or customer evidence; otherwise label them hypotheses and identify a validation step.

Not observed is not absent. An unlisted feature or missing public price gets an unavailable/not-listed finding dated to the source, not a false exclusion or a zero-dollar price. Do not infer a competitor's motive from a site change. Keep facts, interpretation, and recommendations separate.

## One live objection

Capture the speaker's actual wording, deal context, buying stage, and supported proof. Identify plausible concerns, ask a question that separates them, and provide a concise response the rep can say as written. Include the conditions under which the offer is a poor fit. Do not promote a one-deal anecdote into a market-wide advantage.

## Win-loss analysis

- Keep wins, competitive losses, no-decision, and churn distinct. Confirm which competitor won; a mention during discovery is not proof of the outcome.
- Code one primary reason per deal, optional secondary reasons, source, supporting quote/note, and confidence. Distinguish capability/fit, budget/value, time to value, trust/risk, relationship/process, and timing/no decision.
- Account for source bias: rep-entered CRM reasons are summaries; calls capture what was said; buyer interviews capture a sampled recollection. Reconcile disagreement with source dates and context.
- Segment by buyer, competitor, deal size, and stage before aggregating. Report counts and denominators and flag small or one-sided samples. Do not make a small-sample percentage sound like a stable win rate.
- Route supported findings to battlecards, discovery questions, objection libraries, and product-gap proposals. Draft updates first and preserve permissions for CRM writes or contacting buyers.
