# Market Sizing

Use for TAM/SAM/SOM and market-entry decisions. Define the buyer, geography, category, price unit, time horizon, and inclusion/exclusion rules first.

- **TAM:** all eligible buyers in the defined market multiplied by plausible annual value per buyer. It is an opportunity estimate, not a revenue forecast.
- **SAM:** the portion the actual offer and operating model can serve, after geographic, segment, regulatory, and delivery constraints.
- **SOM:** the portion realistically obtainable within the stated horizon, constrained by reachable buyers, acquisition economics, conversion, retention, capacity, and competition.

Triangulate at least two genuinely independent methods when evidence permits:

1. Bottom-up: eligible account/customer counts and annual spend; prefer current primary data and a bounded price range.
2. Search-led: observed demand for buying queries, with duplicates, intent, channel coverage, and query-to-buyer assumptions explicit. Searches are not unique customers.
3. Competitor-led: verified competitor revenue/customers by segment; expose gaps and avoid adding overlapping markets.
4. Channel-led: reachable qualified buyers, acquisition capacity, and attainable revenue; useful as a constraint on SOM.

Label input evidence and calculated outputs separately. Show low/base/high scenarios with their source dates and sensitive assumptions. Reconcile disagreement rather than averaging incompatible scopes. Report unavailable data honestly.

Evaluate attractiveness using demand, urgency, willingness to pay, reachable channels, differentiation, competitive intensity, retention, margin, sales-cycle burden, and delivery capacity. If scoring, define the scale and weights and label the result as a planning heuristic. A large TAM alone does not establish a viable entry strategy.

Return the scope, formulas/units, input sources, scenario table, uncertainty, binding constraints, and a next validation action. Do not present an assumed market share as measured traction.
