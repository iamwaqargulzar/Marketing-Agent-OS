# Product context, voice, and learnings

Maintain context only where the user authorizes it. Use one registry writer; other skills propose changes rather than silently overwriting shared truth.

Derive voice from actual writing, approved customer language, and relevant performance evidence. Describe usable attributes with real examples, boundaries, context-dependent tone, preferred vocabulary, and prohibited language with reasons. A founder sample or explicit brief can outweigh generic style heuristics; do not invent a persona or claim an untested line performed.

When relevant, add an optional paid-media brief: goal, account scope, measured target/floor, budget and pacing, conversions/value/bidding role, known tracking weaknesses, and exact permitted actions. Do not infer missing targets from industry averages or convert a strategy preference into spend approval.

For a learning, record date, scope/cohort/channel, supported finding or user decision, source/sample/window, uncertainty, owner, and active/watch/retired state. Distinguish a measured result from a provisional observation and a user preference. A result from another audience or stale product state may not apply.

Propose a learning after a test/campaign/postmortem, but obtain permission before updating the registry unless that exact write is already authorized. Version substantive changes and preserve history. Retire superseded claims with a reason instead of silently deleting conflicting evidence; set review cadence and size to the team's needs rather than an arbitrary universal row limit.

Drafting skills should consult relevant approved learnings before re-proposing a settled idea. A new test may be justified by changed evidence or scope; prior results are not permanent universal rules.
