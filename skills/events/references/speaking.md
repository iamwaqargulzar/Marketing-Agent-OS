# Event Speaking

Use this reference to pursue speaking opportunities, design a talk, and reuse the resulting content.

## Get booked

- Frame the proposal around the audience's problem and takeaway, not product promotion.
- Make the title specific to audience, outcome, and tension.
- Keep the abstract concise: problem, concrete takeaways, and verifiable speaker relevance.
- Build a portfolio from authorized recordings and prior sessions.
- Confirm organizer deadlines, selection rules, travel support, recording rights, and disclosure requirements from current first-party information.

Submitting a CFP, contacting an organizer, or accepting terms is an external action and requires explicit authorization.

## Design the talk

Before slides, define:

1. one audience and what they already believe;
2. the practical takeaway;
3. the speaker or business outcome;
4. a small set of points, each supported by verified evidence or experience;
5. the emotional and attention arc;
6. the final next step and disclosure.

Every quantitative or comparative claim needs a source or a clear evidence label. Remove unsupported proof rather than polishing it.

## Design for the recording

Important ideas must work in audio and transcript form, not only visually. Speak key definitions, framework names, and verified figures. Provide captions, readable slides, alt text or descriptions where appropriate, and a durable URL for authorized supporting material.

Confirm permission to record, republish, quote attendees, and reuse organizer branding before production or publication.

## Compound the asset

With authorization, turn the session into a written article, accessible transcript, short clips, social excerpts, sales proof, FAQ research, and an improved next version of the talk. Preserve context around claims and do not manufacture endorsements from attendee questions or reactions.
